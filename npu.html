📖 What is an NPU?

NPUs are dedicated AI accelerators. Unlike general-purpose CPUs or graphics-focused GPUs, NPUs are designed specifically for the matrix math required for deep learning and neural network inference.

While CPUs are optimized for low-latency serial processing, and GPUs for highly parallel graphical pixel rendering, NPUs are optimized for dense matrix multiplication and convolution operations. They execute low-precision math (like INT8, FP16, BF16) at staggering speeds, dramatically reducing the time and power required to train or infer with large AI models.

💡 Research Fact

Modern NPUs use systolic arrays, a hardware architecture where data flows through a grid of arithmetic logic units (ALUs) in a pipelined fashion, minimizing the need to fetch data from memory repeatedly. This makes them up to 100x more efficient per watt than CPUs for AI workloads.

📊 Key Research Papers & Resources

To truly understand NPUs, diving into academic literature is essential:

  • In-Datacenter Performance Analysis of a Tensor Processing Unit - Jouppi et al. (Google, 2017) - The foundational paper introducing Google's TPU architecture.
  • Systolic Array Architectures - Kung & Leiserson (1978) - The mathematical and structural foundation for modern AI chips.
  • Hardware for Machine Learning: Challenges and Opportunities - Sze et al. (2020) - Comprehensive overview of the NPU landscape.

📜 History

AI acceleration started with GPUs, but Google's TPU (2016) and Apple's Neural Engine (2017) proved that dedicated silicon could perform AI tasks much faster and with less power.
