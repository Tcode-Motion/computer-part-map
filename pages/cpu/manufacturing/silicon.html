Silicon — The Foundation of Digital Computing

Silicon (Si, element 14) is the semiconductor material used in virtually all computer chips. "Silicon Valley" is named after it.

“– Why Silicon?

Silicon is a semiconductor — it can act as both a conductor and insulator depending on conditions. This property makes it ideal for building transistors. It's also the second most abundant element in Earth's crust (27.7%), making it cheap and plentiful.

§ª Doping — Making Silicon Useful

Pure silicon is a poor conductor. By adding tiny amounts of other elements (doping), we change its electrical properties:

Doping TypeDopantEffectResult
N-typePhosphorus (P), Arsenic (As)Adds extra electrons (negative carriers)More free electrons = better conductor
P-typeBoron (B), Gallium (Ga)Creates "holes" (positive carriers)Holes act as positive charge carriers

A P-N junction (where P-type meets N-type) is the basis of all diodes and transistors — the fundamental building blocks of every CPU.
