Transistors — The Atoms of Computing

A transistor is an electronic switch that can be either ON (1) or OFF (0). Modern CPUs contain billions of them, each just a few nanometers wide.

“– What is a Transistor?

A transistor is a tiny electronic switch made from semiconductor material (silicon). It has three terminals — Gate, Source, and Drain. By applying voltage to the Gate, you control whether current flows from Source to Drain (ON = 1) or not (OFF = 0).

By combining billions of these switches in complex arrangements, CPUs can perform arithmetic, logic, and memory operations that power all of digital computing.

’¡ Did You Know?

The transistor was invented at Bell Labs in 1947 by John Bardeen, Walter Brattain, and William Shockley. They won the Nobel Prize in Physics (1956) for this discovery — one of the most consequential inventions in human history.
