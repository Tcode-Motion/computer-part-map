📖 What is a Graphics Card?

A graphics card (also called a discrete GPU or video card) is an expansion card that installs into a PCIe slot on the motherboard. It has its own dedicated GPU chip, VRAM, power delivery, and cooling — offloading all rendering tasks from the CPU and integrated graphics.

Modern graphics cards include hardware ray tracing units, dedicated AI tensor cores (NVIDIA) or matrix accelerators (AMD), and support technologies like DLSS 3 and FSR 3 for AI-powered upscaling.

💡 Did You Know?

The NVIDIA RTX 4090 has 16,384 CUDA cores and 76 billion transistors. It's so powerful that it can render 60fps at 8K resolution in some games — a resolution most monitors don't even support yet.
