📖 What is Quantum Computing?

Quantum computers use qubits instead of bits. This allows them to solve specific complex problems—like molecular simulation or breaking RSA encryption—exponentially faster than any classical supercomputer.

This allows quantum computers to perform massive multi-dimensional computations at once, scaling exponentially. While a classical 100-bit state represents exactly one of $2^{100}$ possible states, a 100-qubit state represents a probability distribution across all $2^{100}$ states simultaneously.

💡 Shor's Algorithm

In 1994, Peter Shor formulated an algorithm that can theoretically factor large prime numbers exponentially faster than any known classical algorithm. If a large enough stable quantum computer is built, it will break modern RSA encryption overnight.

🔬 Core Quantum Principles

PrincipleScientific DefinitionComputational Impact
SuperpositionA quantum system remains in a linear combination of multiple states until measured.Allows processing of a vast number of possibilities simultaneously without extra time.
EntanglementParticles become inextricably linked; the state of one instantly dictates the state of another, regardless of distance.Enables complex correlation of data points and exponentially scales computational power per qubit.
DecoherenceThe loss of quantum coherence (state) due to interaction with the environment (heat, radiation).The biggest hurdle in building quantum computers; requires cooling systems to near absolute zero (-273°C).

🧪 Hardware Approaches to Qubits

Superconducting Circuits

Loops of superconducting wire cooled to near absolute zero. Used by IBM & Google.

Fastest gates, but high error rates.

Trapped Ions

Individual ionized atoms trapped by electromagnetic fields and controlled by lasers. (IonQ)

Highly stable, longer coherence times.

Photonic Qubits

Using single particles of light (photons) travelling through waveguides. (PsiQuantum)

Can operate at room temperature.

Topological Qubits

Theoretical approach encoding data in braided paths of anyons. (Microsoft research)

Theoretically immune to decoherence.

📜 History

Theoretical foundations laid in the 1980s by Richard Feynman and David Deutsch. Google claimed "Quantum Supremacy" in 2019 with its 53-qubit Sycamore processor.

🏗️ Architecture

Built using superconducting loops, trapped ions, or photonic circuits. Requires extreme environments, often cooled to near absolute zero (-273°C).
